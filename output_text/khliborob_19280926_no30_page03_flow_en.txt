PETYA'S SYSTEM.
Command of the Ukrainian News.
One day, a deputation of women came to me when I was a teacher in Doryzoni. The point was that these women wanted to send their children to school, but Petya from Gadzymkovyts did not allow it. So the women thought that perhaps there was some personal misunderstanding between me and Petya, and they came to resolve it. The head of the deputation, Boichykha from the First Colony, was surprised when I said that I had never even seen Petya.
'Oh, that's a pity,' she lamented, 'you should go to him, they are so good, they have such an angelic disposition, you will surely love them.'
From further conversation, I learned that Petya, if not Christ incarnate, at least walks somewhere near that. There was no way out: I promised Boichykha that at the first opportunity I would visit that angelic nature. Soon after that conversation, I found a horse and went with reverence to the modern Christ. I found him just as he stood at the gate, arms outstretched on both sides, while the women and children who worked in his field (meaning: for God), already going home, each approached one or the other; and whoever arrived in time, when it was not yet crowded, licked both.
Having learned who I was, Petya let the rest of the women go home without 'kissing,' and we went to talk quietly in the house. There we talked, as they say, heart to heart, for four hours we pounded like a drum, and parted with the same convictions with which we met. To recount the entire conversation, which took place 14 years ago, is not worth it, but its essence can be conveyed in short sentences. It was supposed to be like this: the modern Christ considered it his sacred duty not to allow a colonist to acquire wealth and go to school. And the reasons were as follows: 'a peasant,' who has a little money in his pocket, immediately begins to think about school for his children; and school generally leads beyond the church. Because 'once a child learns to read, he grabs any books and ceases to be a good Catholic.' The more we talked on that topic, the more we argued, the clearer it became to me that such a stance — not to allow a 'peasant' to acquire wealth and education — is a system that has a solid foundation. As a Trans-Dnieper Ukrainian, I did not know Galician relations and understood little why it should be beneficial for someone for our colonist here in Brazil to be both poor and ignorant? For really: I argued to Petya that the whole of Southern Germany is Catholic, but no one thought of keeping people away from education and property. A French peasant would let himself be cut up for a Catholic priest; but it does not occur to the priest himself to cement that love by the price of darkness and destitution of his parishioners. Petya argued to me that 'our peasant' is something else; there must be something unusual, who must be under strict supervision, so that a book does not accidentally fall on his shelf or in his drawer. Why 'our peasant' must lead exactly such a life, which differs little from that of cattle, Petya did not tell me. But from all the conversation and his further behavior, it was clear that he had firm instructions regarding 'our peasant,' and he would not deviate from those instructions by a step.

At that time, the vernal equinox fell on March 11 according to the Julian calendar, according to the Nicene calendar it had not yet begun, and it was suspended only when another 11 minutes and 56 seconds had passed. From such a delay, over 1300 years, one full day will arise, and by our time that delay has grown to 13 days. So that's how the Julian calendar differs from the Gregorian!
Now, based on historical data, I will try to explain what prompted Pope Gregory XIII to proceed with the reform of the Julian calendar.
At the time when Julius Caesar introduced his calendar (46 BC), the vernal equinox fell on March 24. And when, in 325 AD, the first ecumenical council gathered in the city of Nicaea, the church fathers, among other things, passed a resolution when Christians are to celebrate the feast of Easter, which, according to apostolic tradition, was to be celebrated on the Sunday after the first vernal full moon, which is the first vernal month. And in the same year, due to the delay of the Julian calendar, the vernal equinox already fell on March 24, and not on March 21.

He was one of the most active members in the vicinity of M. Maliet; he was a subscriber to 'Ukr. May this earth be light for him, and may his life be an example for other Ukrainians – how to live and work for the people.

Important for Ukrainians from Romania.
In the 'Ukr. Holos' newspaper, an explanation was published by Dr. Ivan Stryiskyi, lawyer and former notary candidate in Kitsman, Bukovyna, regarding the provisions of the Romanian constitutional law on civil rights and belonging to the community. Given that there are many Bessarabians in Brazil, we present them in full.
I consider it my duty to draw attention to our settlers from Romania, that the Romanian government is now beginning to implement the constitutional law, and specifically Article 18, which states that in Romania, only those who are Romanian citizens can own land in villages, meaning those who are registered in the community lists of citizens. This Article 18 of the constitution states literally: Only Romanians or naturalized Romanians can acquire any title and hold land in villages in Romania. Foreigners will only have the right to the value of these
land in villages. And Romania currently only those who are registered in the list; naturalized is he who is admitted to citizenship, even if he is not from Romania by birth. Now, very strict regulations have been issued and sent to all authorities, public administrations, prosecutor's offices, and courts, to make lists of all those who are not registered as citizens, because their land will be taken away in the villages, and they will be given its value, but what value and when – that is not yet known.
Therefore, I consider it my duty to advise all our settlers to apply in writing either to the Romanian consul or to their communities in the country (but through a lawyer familiar with the matter, and in the Romanian language, because otherwise applications are not accepted), so that they can be registered in the lists. The Romanian consul can also register them in the lists and issue a certificate that must be well hidden. It is also important that in the country, every citizen receives a certificate of belonging, which, according to Romanian law, is issued only once. And if it is lost, then with great difficulty, because there are difficult bureaucratic procedures to obtain a duplicate of that certificate of belonging. In contracts of any title, this is required.
