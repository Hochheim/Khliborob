The newspaper 'Ukrainian Farmer', issue 11 (139) from March 16, 1927, presents diverse news from Brazil, Ukraine, and around the world. From Brazil, reports cover the arrival of Spanish and upcoming Polish emigrants to São Paulo, a violent incident at the American consulate, and general issues of trade and illiteracy. Ukrainian affairs include protests by scholars against the oppression of Belarusians and Ukrainians in Poland, the discovery of historical documents related to Hetman Mazepa in Paris, and the liquidation of 'khrunivstvo' (a political movement) by Bachynsky. International news covers the political isolation of Yugoslavia, England's role in the Balkans, Pope Pius XI's awards to Polish figures, and the situation in Upper Silesia. The issue also includes a chronicle of life in Brazil, financial reports, and editorial correspondence. The first step for a farmer in cultivating corn is to select suitable cobs, and from those cobs — the proper grain for seed. Good and careful selection of cobs for seed is practically very important, and we will discuss this in this article. When you look at the cobs, you will see that some of them are even, not twisted. Such even ones should be selected from the pile, and twisted ones should not be taken for seed under any circumstances. When we further examine the selected even cobs, we will see that some of them, from the bottom to the top, are almost uniformly thick and densely covered with seeds from one end to the other, while others thin towards the top and either have no seeds at all, or their seeds are shriveled, underdeveloped. Again, for seed, we will only take the first cobs, densely packed with grain. And having selected them, we proceed to one review of them, namely, — we examine whether the grain on the cob is not damaged, and from the cobs with whole grain, we select only those where the grain is long. This is important because then a larger percentage (%) of grain will be on the cob, or in other words, more corn grain will yield from each morgen. When the selection of cobs is finished, this is not the end of the matter; it is not yet possible to remove the grain from the cob; it is still necessary to test each cob for germination strength, so that for sowing only those cobs are taken whose grain grows vigorously, does not get sick, and therefore, will transmit this vigor to the entire stem in posterity. For this purpose, the cobs selected for seed are arranged in order and the grains are removed, starting from the first, 6 grains from each cob. They are removed as follows: with the tip of a small knife, one grain is removed from the upper part of the cob, then the cob is turned halfway, and one grain is removed from the middle part, then again turned to one-third and again one grain is removed, but this time from the lower part of the cob. Shelled and placed grains on cell no. 1, on the canvas. The same on cell no. 3, etc. and later so that the cobs do not get mixed up. Shelled and placed, then it is put into a jar with 3 jars of water until it reaches the grains, filled. Also pour off and place with a small board, and keep it level. corn grain, long root. This way, they will shell only the necessary amount of grain, and the lower, discarded grain from the first and second ends. This way, the grain comes out with a certain, mature, full, healthy development. (From 'Silsʹky Svit'). V. Samoilovich. READERS' CORNER. M. Mallet, March 14. 'Good day, esteemed 'Khliborob'!'
