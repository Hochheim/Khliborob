ATTACKS WITHOUT END OR MEASURE.

Simultaneously with complex issues of internal politics, with the concerns of everyday life and with great urgent material hardship, new Basilian branches struck us. It is necessary to particularly note the Basilian method in such an unfair struggle with their opponent.

This struggle, conducted by cultural methods for the good of our emigration, is not an evil, and even the name of electing and securing better living conditions for our common interest cannot be treated as something for individuals when it threatens their relation to the whole. Generally, private interests do not have a purpose, and no special attention can be paid to this when it is national.

Your son is alive and well. Myroslava, the most bloody daughter and the battlefield covered with corpses, and the boyar's house, surrounded by a cloud of Mongols. There was no doubt, all the young men sent for the gathering perished in an unequal struggle with those invaders. Not knowing what to do, the Tukhol youth returned to the village, spreading terrible news everywhere. Hearing it, old Zakhar trembled, and a bitter tear rolled down his old face. 'My dream has come true!' — he whispered. — 'In defense of his village, my Maksym fell. That's how it should be. Everyone dies once, but not everyone gets to die gloriously. I should not mourn him, but rejoice in his fate.' 'Allow me, honest father,' Myroslava said, as she approached him, trembling from inner excitement, ignoring everyone. She lifted him, afraid that something would happen. She asked him to listen. But he did not answer. A girl ran up from somewhere and comforted him, but her words did not help. Zakhar got up and left, he did not look at her, because he did not want her to see his tears. He went to the forest. Old Zakhar still comforted himself, that his heart ached deeply: too strongly, with all the strength of his soul, he loved his youngest son. But he quickly cried out. The community called him, needed his advice. Thousands of people, old and young, for the village, for the Tukhol region, for which their terrible enemy stood so close. For the first time since Bach Tukholya was founded, the community council gathered today without usual rites, without banners, amidst the clang of axes and scythes, amidst a half-alarmed, two-paved military clamor. In disorder, elders mingled with young men, armed with unarmed, and even women bustled back and forth among the crowd, asking for news about the enemy or loudly mourning their fallen sons.

'What to do? What to start with? How to fight?' — roared in the crowd. One idea prevailed over all others: to go out as a community and fight the Mongols to the last drop of blood. The youth especially insisted on this. 'We want to die like our brothers who died in defense of our land!' — they shouted. — 'Only over our corpses will the enemies enter the Tukhol valley!' 'In the narrow pass, make barricades and defeat the Mongols!' — advised the elders. Then, when the noise subsided a little, Zakhar Berkut began to speak.
